# The production access questions

The form as it looked *(checked 2026-10)*: ten questions, free text capped at 300 characters, two of them selects. Google may reword, add or drop questions; answer whatever the current form asks with the same principle.

The principle: the reviewer is checking whether real people used a pre-release build and whether the developer changed the app because of them. An answer either shows that with a number or wastes the space.

**How did you recruit testers?** Name each group and the channel used to reach it. Declare paid testers.

**How easy was it to recruit?** A select. Answer honestly; "difficult" fits a new app and agrees with paid testers.

**Describe tester engagement.** Numbers from analytics, never opt-ins: distinct tester devices over the window, the daily range, how many were active on most days, how many took an update from Play, and what they did per feature. Quote Play's own active-device figure too if it agrees.

**Summarise feedback and how you collected it.** Channels, then specific complaints with devices or versions. Specific beats positive.

**Who is the audience?** Who, where, and on what devices. Saying what devices they use connects the feedback to the fixes.

**How does the app provide value?** What a user does in it today, in their words, with one number. Only features in the build under review.

**Expected installs in the first year?** A select. Pick the honest band. An inflated estimate buys nothing.

**What did you change because of testing?** One sentence per change: the feedback, the version that answered it, and the evidence it worked. Map every complaint from the feedback answer to a line here.

**How did you decide the app is ready?** Builds shipped in the window, fixes, devices tested on, the crash record from tester devices, and the share of testers who kept updating.

**What did you do differently?** (repeat applications) Name the previous mistake plainly, then the change.

Cutting to the limit: drop adjectives first, then the second example of anything. Keep the numbers.
